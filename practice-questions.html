Question 1 · GA License Law & Rules
Before revoking a license for cause, the Commission must generally provide:
  • A. A commission bonus
  • B. A new listing
  • C. A tax refund
  • D. Notice and an opportunity for a hearing
Show answer & explanation
Answer: D

Due process requires the Commission to give the licensee notice and an opportunity for a hearing before imposing serious sanctions like revocation.

Question 2 · GA License Law & Rules
Before the Recovery Fund will pay a claim, the aggrieved person must first:
  • A. Ask the broker nicely
  • B. File a form with the MLS
  • C. Wait five years
  • D. Obtain a court judgment against the licensee
Show answer & explanation
Answer: D

A claimant must first obtain a court judgment against the licensee before seeking payment from the Recovery Fund.

Question 3 · Ownership & Title
A tenant who stays after the lease ends, without permission, holds an:
  • A. Estate for years
  • B. Periodic estate
  • C. Estate at will
  • D. Estate at sufferance
Show answer & explanation
Answer: D

An estate at sufferance arises when a tenant remains in possession after the lease ends, without the landlord's permission.

Question 4 · Ownership & Title
The legal process that proves the validity of a will and oversees distribution of a decedent's estate is called:
  • A. Escheat
  • B. Dedication
  • C. Alienation
  • D. Probate
Show answer & explanation
Answer: D

Probate is the court-supervised process that validates a will (or handles an intestate estate), settles debts, and distributes the decedent's property.

Question 5 · Land Use & Disclosures
When a land-use regulation goes so far that it deprives an owner of essentially all economic use of the land, it may be challenged as a:
  • A. Legal nonconforming use
  • B. Regulatory taking
  • C. Buffer zone
  • D. Special use
Show answer & explanation
Answer: B

A regulation that removes nearly all economically viable use of a property can amount to a regulatory taking, for which the owner may seek compensation.

Question 6 · Land Use & Disclosures
A broker who knowingly repeats a seller's false statement about the property:
  • A. Has no liability because it was the seller's statement
  • B. Is fully protected by the as-is clause
  • C. Can be held liable for the misrepresentation
  • D. Is liable only to the seller, not the buyer
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

A broker who knows a statement is false and repeats it to a buyer can be personally liable for misrepresentation; the seller's origin of the statement is no defense.

Question 7 · Contracts
Two parties orally agree to sell a house, but the seller backs out. The agreement is:
  • A. Enforceable in court
  • B. Unenforceable under the statute of frauds
  • C. A criminal breach
  • D. Automatically recorded
Show answer & explanation
Answer: B

An oral agreement to sell real estate falls within the statute of frauds and is unenforceable if a party refuses to perform.

Question 8 · Contracts
Before closing, a fire totally destroys the only structure that was the subject of the contract. Performance may be excused by:
  • A. Novation
  • B. Assignment
  • C. Impossibility of performance
  • D. Ratification
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

Destruction of the unique subject matter can make performance objectively impossible, which may discharge the contract by impossibility.

Question 9 · Agency & Property Management
Which statement about real estate agency relationships is TRUE?
  • A. A listing agent represents the buyer
  • B. Dual agency needs no consent
  • C. A buyer's agent owes the buyer fiduciary duties
  • D. Who pays always determines representation
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

A buyer's agent owes the buyer fiduciary duties. A listing agent represents the seller, dual agency needs consent, and payment does not determine representation.

Question 10 · Agency & Property Management
In managing a property under a management agreement, the property manager usually acts as the owner's:
  • A. Special agent
  • B. General agent
  • C. Subagent of the tenant
  • D. Dual agent
Show answer & explanation
Answer: B

A property manager is typically a general agent, authorized to handle a range of ongoing tasks for the owner, unlike a special agent hired for one specific act.

Question 11 · Real Estate Practice
The 1988 Fair Housing Amendments added protection for familial status and:
  • A. Disability
  • B. Age
  • C. Income
  • D. Occupation
Show answer & explanation
Answer: A

The 1988 Fair Housing Amendments Act added familial status and disability (handicap) as protected classes.

Question 12 · Real Estate Practice
Two firms agreeing to refuse to work with a discount broker is an illegal:
  • A. Group boycott
  • B. Listing agreement
  • C. Referral arrangement
  • D. Open house
Show answer & explanation
Answer: A

Competitors conspiring to refuse to deal with a particular broker is an illegal group boycott.

Question 13 · Financing
A large final payment due at the end of a partially amortized loan is a:
  • A. Down payment
  • B. Discount point
  • C. Balloon payment
  • D. Escrow deposit
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

A balloon payment is a large lump-sum payment due at the end of a loan that was not fully amortized.

Question 14 · Financing
Pushing a qualified borrower into a costlier, worse loan can be a form of:
  • A. Predatory lending
  • B. Fair lending
  • C. Good customer service
  • D. Careful underwriting
Show answer & explanation
Answer: A

Steering a borrower who qualifies for better terms into a costlier loan is a predatory lending practice.

Question 15 · Valuation & Math
An appraiser judges a well-maintained 30-year-old home to be in the condition of a typical 15-year-old home. The 15-year figure is the home's:
  • A. Actual age
  • B. Chronological age
  • C. Remaining economic life
  • D. Effective age
Show answer & explanation
Answer: D

Effective age reflects a building's condition and usefulness rather than the years since it was built. Good upkeep can make effective age lower than actual (chronological) age.

Question 16 · Valuation & Math
A loan origination fee differs from a discount point in that the origination fee:
  • A. Lowers the loan's interest rate
  • B. Is always exactly one point
  • C. Covers the lender's cost of processing the loan
  • D. Is paid only by the seller
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

An origination fee compensates the lender for making and processing the loan. A discount point, by contrast, is paid to buy down the interest rate.

Question 17 · GA Real Estate Practice
A firm proposes to have one broker personally represent both the buyer and the seller in the same sale. This is permissible only if:
  • A. The transaction is residential
  • B. The commission is split evenly
  • C. Both clients give written consent to the dual agency
  • D. The buyer and seller never meet in person
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

Under BRRETA (O.C.G.A. §10-6A-12), a single licensee representing both parties is dual agency, permitted only with the written consent of both clients. The consent must disclose the nature of the arrangement and confirm it is voluntary.

Question 18 · GA Real Estate Practice
A listing broker gets a full-price offer but hopes to find a buyer of their own first. The broker must:
  • A. Present the offer and act in the seller's interest
  • B. Delay presenting the full-price offer for now
  • C. Quietly reject the offer to keep looking
  • D. Steer the seller toward waiting longer
Show answer & explanation
Answer: A

Loyalty requires the broker to present the offer promptly and act in the seller's interest, not delay it for the broker's chance to earn both sides of the commission.

Question 19 · GA Finance & Closing
Under Georgia's title-theory approach, legal title during the loan is held by the:
  • A. County government
  • B. Buyer's agent
  • C. Lender
  • D. Closing attorney
Show answer & explanation
Answer: C

In a title-theory state like Georgia, the lender holds legal title under the security deed until the borrower repays the loan.

Question 20 · GA Finance & Closing
The transfer tax attaches to the deed, while the intangibles tax attaches to the:
  • A. Security instrument
  • B. Listing agreement
  • C. Property tax bill
  • D. Title insurance policy
Show answer & explanation
Answer: A

The transfer tax is on the deed conveying title; the intangible recording tax is on the security instrument securing the loan.
