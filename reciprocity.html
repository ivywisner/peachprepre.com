Georgia Real Estate Reciprocity: Every State Qualifies (With One Exception)

The Short Answer

Does Georgia Have Real Estate Reciprocity?

🤝
Yes — With Every State
GREC's own words: "Georgia offers licensing by reciprocity to licensees from all states." Not a short list of partner states, not a watered-down "mutual recognition" deal — all of them. Most states maintain a careful little roster of who they'll trade licenses with. Georgia skipped the roster.

Here's what that means in practice. If you hold an active real estate license from another state, you can apply for the equivalent Georgia license without taking the Georgia exam and without sitting through the 75-hour pre-license course. Two conditions, straight from the Georgia Real Estate Commission:

  1. You earned the license by passing that state's exam. A license you picked up through someone else's reciprocity deal doesn't count — Georgia wants proof you passed a real licensing exam somewhere, not a chain of waivers.
  2. The license is currently in good standing — not lapsed, not expired, not suspended. If you let your old license die two years ago, there's nothing for Georgia to reciprocate with.

Meet both, and the path is paperwork: you complete GREC's Real Estate Reciprocal Application, read the instructions carefully, and include the required attachments (your home-state licensure certification is the big one). That's the whole game — for 49 states' worth of agents, there is no exam at the end of it.

✓ Why this is a bigger deal than it sounds

Compare the neighbors. Florida recognizes exactly 10 states and makes everyone else start over. Many states only waive the national portion and still make you test on state law. Georgia waives everything — course and exam — for licensees from every state. If you're an experienced agent relocating to Atlanta, Savannah, or anywhere between, Georgia has quietly built one of the easiest license transfers in the country.

One caution: rules like this can change, and the application's instructions control the details. Confirm on GREC's site before you plan a move around it.
The Exception

The Florida Rule — One State Gets Homework

There's exactly one asterisk on "all states," and it's shaped like Florida. Per GREC: licensed Florida residents must pass a supplemental exam before Georgia will issue them a license — and that supplemental is the Georgia portion of the state exam. That's the same 52-question Georgia section every new licensee faces, covering Georgia license law, GREC rules, BRRETA, security deeds — the purely Georgia half of the material. You'd schedule it through PSI (their Candidate Information Bulletin has the process), and the bar is the same 75% that applies to everyone.

Why single out Florida? Georgia doesn't explain itself, but the symmetry is hard to miss: Florida is famous for not simply accepting anyone's license either — it makes Georgia agents (and everyone else on its mutual recognition list) pass a 40-question Florida law exam before working Florida deals. Each state wants proof you actually know its law before handing you a license. Fair's fair.

💡

Don't let "only the state portion" fool you. Those 52 questions are concentrated, pure Georgia material — the exact content an out-of-state agent has never studied. No national-principles questions to pad your score with, and the same 75% pass bar. Coming from Florida, the Georgia supplement isn't a formality; it's the entire test, distilled to the part you don't know yet.

Everyone Else

Not Licensed Anywhere Yet? Here's Your Path

Reciprocity is a transfer program, not a shortcut for new agents. If you don't already hold a license from another state, you take the standard Georgia route: the 75-hour pre-license course, then the 152-question PSI exam — 100 national questions plus the same 52-question Georgia portion, passed at 75% on each portion separately. The good news: Georgia's version of "standard" moves faster than almost any state's, and we've mapped the whole thing.

Going the Other Way

Does a Georgia License Travel Well?

Better than most. A Georgia license earned by exam is exactly what other states' reciprocity and recognition programs ask for — Florida, for instance, lists Georgia among the 10 states it grants mutual recognition, so a Georgia agent can add a Florida license by passing just Florida's 40-question law exam. Every destination state sets its own rules, so check with that state's commission — but "licensed in Georgia, passed the Georgia exam" is a strong hand to hold almost anywhere.

Reciprocity rules verified October 2026 against the Georgia Real Estate Commission's official How to Obtain a License page (salesperson section): reciprocity with all states, exam-passage and good-standing requirements, and the Florida supplemental exam rule.
