Short Answer
Most people go from zero to licensed Georgia salesperson in about 2–4 months. Nearly all of that is the 75-hour pre-license course. Everything after it moves in days: a same-day GCIC background report, a PSI exam with your score on screen the moment you finish, and a license that can be issued at the test center itself.
The Long Pole

The 75-Hour Course Sets Your Pace

Georgia's required pre-license course is 75 instructional hours at a Commission-approved school, and it dominates the timeline. Studying part-time around a job, most students take 4–8 weeks to finish; self-paced online formats can move faster if you have the hours to give. The course ends with its own final exam, so the last stretch is study time either way.

One eligibility quirk worth knowing if you're starting young: you can sit the licensing exam at 17 — but the license itself can't be activated until you turn 18.

The Fast Middle

Paperwork and the Exam Move in Days

Once the course is done, the remaining steps are quick. The GCIC criminal history report is a same-day errand at most sheriff's offices or police stations — just don't run it too early, since it must be no more than 60 days old when you apply. Scheduling the PSI exam happens online or by phone, with test centers around metro Atlanta and across the state; how soon you test mostly depends on seat availability. And there's no waiting on results — your score appears on screen the moment you finish.

The Finish Line

Georgia Skips the Waiting Room

In most states, passing the exam starts another clock: mail the application, wait out the processing queue, refresh the licensing portal. Georgia mostly skips that. PSI can issue your license right at the test center shortly after you pass — they say to allow 24 hours — and if you bring a completed Sponsoring Broker Statement, it's issued active and you're ready to work under your broker. Without one, it's issued inactive until you affiliate.

The wildcards that can stretch the timeline: a background investigation if your GCIC report shows anything other than NO RECORD (the Commission calls the process extensive and doesn't put a number on it), retakes if the exam doesn't go your way ($175 and a few days each), and the two 12-month clocks — each exam fee is good for one year, and once you pass, you have 12 months to apply before you'd have to retest.

The Bottom Line

The Course Sets the Pace — Everything Else Is Days

If you want the license sooner, the only lever that really matters is how fast you move through the 75 hours. Georgia won't slow you down after that: same-day background report, instant exam results, and a license that can be in your hands before some states would have opened your envelope. Study well once, and the calendar takes care of itself.
