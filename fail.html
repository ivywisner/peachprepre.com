First Things First

Right After the Exam

You don't leave wondering. Your score appears on screen the moment you finish, and a score report lands in your email — printable anytime from your PSI account.

And here's the part that matters in Georgia: the exam is two separate portions — a 100-question national portion and a 52-question Georgia supplement — each scored on its own, each with its own 75% bar. Your emailed report shows a diagnostic breakdown of your strengths and weaknesses by content area, so you walk out knowing exactly what sank you, not just that something did.

Now the good news: you only retake the portion you failed. Passed the national but the Georgia supplement got you? You redo just the supplement — GREC license law, BRRETA, finance and closing — not the whole four-hour sitting. The full format is broken down in our exam format guide.

💡

Don't delete that score report. The content-area breakdown is basically a custom study plan for round two — it's telling you where your points leaked out. Use it to decide what to study, instead of starting over from scratch.

Step by Step

The Retake Process

Here's the whole path from "I failed" to back in the testing chair:

  1. Rebook with PSI — online or by phone. You can't book a new appointment the same day you tested, but the turnaround is fast: PSI's own example is a candidate who fails on Wednesday, calls Thursday, and retests as soon as Friday, depending on space.
  2. Pay the exam fee again. Every attempt is $175, even when you're only retaking one portion — there's no discount, which is exactly why the retake you prepare for properly is the last one you pay for.
  3. Study the gaps, not everything. Your diagnostic report already told you which content areas you missed. Pour your hours there instead of re-reading what you already nailed. (This is exactly what PeachPrep RE organizes for you — questions grouped by topic, with missed-question review.)
  4. Retake only the failed portion, see your score on the spot again, and move on with your license.
Don't Miss This

Georgia's Fine Print

Two clocks are worth respecting. First, each $175 exam fee is good for one year — if you don't test within a year of paying, the fee is forfeited. Second, once you do pass, you have 12 months to apply for your license; wait longer and you're retesting. PSI's bulletin sets no cap on the number of attempts — the only real limits are your calendar and your patience for paying $175.

🎖️

Veterans: Georgia has a rule most people never hear about. If you're an honorably discharged veteran with at least one year of active duty including 90 days during wartime, you may qualify for veteran's preference points — applied only after a failing score. Mail your original score report and a copy of your DD-214 to PSI within 60 days; if the points push you over the line, you're issued a pass. Details are in Rule 520-1-.04(3) and the PSI candidate bulletin.

The Bottom Line

You're Closer Than It Feels

Failing the Georgia exam isn't the end of the road — it's a checkpoint with a map attached. You get a precise breakdown of what to fix, you probably only have to retake part of it, and you can be back in the chair before the week is out.

The people who pass round two do three things: they target the content areas their report flagged, they put in a high volume of practice questions, and they take at least one full timed run-through before going back. That turns the retake from a rematch into a formality.
